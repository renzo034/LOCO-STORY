Loren and Joco

The first hello

Loren did not plan to notice Joco. It was an ordinary Monday, an ordinary classroom, and a seat that just happened to be empty beside the window.

Joco sat down, smiled, and said hello like it was the easiest thing in the world. Loren said hello back a little too late, and the whole day felt different after that.

Joco was funny without trying to be. Joco listened with full attention, laughed with the whole face, and never made anyone feel small. By Friday, Loren was already looking forward to Monday.

Little things

Weeks passed. They shared lunches, shared playlists, and shared the long walk home when the rain came without warning.

Joco: Did you eat yet?

Loren: Not yet. Waiting for you.

Joco: Haha, you're the best. Save me a seat.

Loren kept every message. A small joke. A photo of a sunset. A voice note that ended with Joco laughing at their own story. To Joco, these were friendly moments. To Loren, each one was a quiet reason to hope.

Somewhere between the rain and the playlists, Loren understood what the feeling was. Loren had fallen for Joco.

The question

Loren practiced the words many times and never said them the same way twice. Every version sounded too small or too loud.

Joco was easy to be around, and that made it harder. If the answer was no, Loren might lose the best friendship of the year. If Loren said nothing, the feeling would only get heavier.

"I would rather be honest and scared than quiet and wondering."

So one evening, on the bench behind the school where they always talked, Loren took a breath and told Joco the truth: I like you. More than a friend.

The answer

Joco did not laugh. Joco did not look away. For a long moment, Joco simply sat with the words, and then spoke gently.

Joco: Loren, thank you for telling me. I know that took courage.

Joco: I'm gay, and I like boys. I can't feel for you the way you hoped, and I don't want to pretend I can.

Loren: I understand.

Joco: You matter to me. I just can't give you what you're asking for.

It hurt. Of course it hurt. Loren's eyes stung, and the bench suddenly felt very quiet.

But beneath the ache, Loren noticed something else. Joco had been kind and honest, and nobody had been tricked. Being turned down was painful. Being lied to would have been worse.

After

The next few days were awkward. Loren kept some distance, and Joco respected it without asking why.

Slowly, the hurt got lighter. Loren learned that liking someone is never a mistake, even when the answer is no. Joco learned that a hard conversation can still be a respectful one.

Loren did not get the love story hoped for. Loren got something quieter: the courage to say what was true, and an honest answer in return.

Some stories end with a kiss. This one ends with two people learning how to be honest with each other.
